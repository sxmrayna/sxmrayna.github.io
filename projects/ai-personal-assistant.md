## Making shared knowledge useful in everyday work

Preparing a briefing, drafting an update or handing over a task often means bringing together reference documents, earlier decisions and current work notes. An organizational brain gives these tasks a shared foundation of knowledge and working instructions.

I design and build workflows that connect this shared context with individual and team responsibilities. Across organizational and venture capital projects, the aim is to reduce repetitive work, make handoffs easier and catch mistakes earlier through AI agents, tool integrations and rule-based automation.

## From stakeholder conversations to workflow requirements

I work with clients and stakeholders to understand how a task is carried out, where people spend time assembling or checking information, and what a useful result needs to contain. I translate those discussions into workflow requirements, expected outputs, review points and handoff responsibilities.

In venture capital work, this meant understanding how investment information arrived, how analysts handled inconsistent spreadsheets and what they needed to verify before using the output. These conversations shaped the requirements for financial data extraction, grouping related records and reconciling entities, as well as where automation needed to leave room for human review.

## Connecting knowledge, AI and existing tools

Turning those requirements into working automation means connecting the team’s knowledge, working files and review responsibilities. I draw on in-depth experience using and developing with Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and AI tools to build agents, scripts and integrations around the way each team works.

My experience covers both everyday use and developing agents and automation around specific business needs. Across these environments, I define how shared knowledge and task-specific context come together, including access boundaries and responsibility for keeping information current.

## Review, validation and reliable handoffs

I assign interpretation, context gathering and drafting to AI, and use defined rules for calculations, required fields, file generation and routing. Making those responsibilities explicit helps keep each step understandable and maintainable.

For workflows involving financial information, I built a code-based validation pipeline to compare AI-generated outputs with the original files before delivery, making discrepancies available for review. I also assess technical feasibility, test incomplete or inconsistent inputs, and compare model options on extraction accuracy, response time and cost. These findings inform implementation decisions and feature priorities.

The illustrative workflow below applies the same principles to a project handoff. An agent drafts a summary from shared references, work notes and open actions. Rules flag missing owners, unclear dates or incomplete fields, so the person handing over the work can resolve those issues before passing it on.
